# Risk Assessment & Risk Control

A risk assessment identifies what could go wrong in your activity, how likely and how serious it is, and what you will do about it. It is the foundation of every other safety plan.

[:material-download: Download the Risk Assessment Template (Excel)](../downloads/risk-assessment-template.xlsx){ .md-button .md-button--primary }

!!! info "Do I have to do one?"
    **Yes.** Under SS 681, sport organisations, clubs, facility operators, event organisers, coaches and instructors are to carry out risk assessments and take reasonably practicable steps to eliminate or reduce foreseeable risks. If you **employ staff, are self-employed, or engage contractors**, risk assessment is also a legal duty under the Workplace Safety and Health (Risk Management) Regulations, and the Code of Practice on WSH Risk Management sets out how to do it. This page follows that Code, so one assessment can serve both purposes.

## The process at a glance

<ol class="ssp-steps">
<li>Prepare</li><li>Identify hazards</li><li>Evaluate risk</li><li>Control risk</li><li>Implement</li><li>Record</li><li>Review</li>
</ol>

*Review leads back to identifying hazards. Communicate with everyone involved at every step.*

## 1. Prepare

- **Form a team.** Involve people who know the activity well: the organiser, a coach or technical official, someone from the venue, and contractors such as the medical provider. A one-person assessment misses things.
- **Appoint a competent leader.** The Code suggests basic competency through a WSQ risk management course or equivalent. For complex or high-risk events, get help from a qualified safety officer or consultant.
- **List the activities.** Break the event into its parts: set-up, registration, warm-up, the activity itself, spectators, transport, take-down. Each becomes a row group in your assessment.
- **Gather information:** the site plan and route, past incident records and debriefs, the competition rules, weather patterns, and any relevant standards.

## 2. Identify hazards

A **hazard** is anything that could cause harm. For each part of the activity, ask "what could go wrong?" and list each hazard on its own line. Consider:

| Category | Examples in sport |
| --- | --- |
| Environment | Heat and humidity, lightning, haze, rain and slippery surfaces, tides and currents, poor lighting |
| People | Fitness and health of participants, age (children, older adults), disabilities, experience level, crowd size and behaviour |
| Activity | Collisions, falls, overexertion, contact and combat, speed, water |
| Equipment and venue | Unsecured goalposts, damaged flooring, faulty gym equipment, temporary structures, vehicles on site |
| Physical, electrical, chemical | Noise, portable generators and cables, pool chemicals, fuel |
| Psychosocial | Fatigue of staff and volunteers, harassment, abuse |
| Systems | No phone signal, no AED nearby, delayed ambulance access |

Also note **who** could be harmed (participants, spectators, staff, volunteers, the public) and **what injury** could result.

## 3. Evaluate the risk

Rate each hazard **with the controls already in place**.

**Severity**: how bad the harm could be.

| Level | Severity | Meaning | Sport example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | Catastrophic | Death, or multiple major injuries | Cardiac arrest with no AED on site; drowning |
| 4 | Major | Serious injury or illness: major fractures, disability, serious head injury | Exertional heatstroke; spinal injury from a fall |
| 3 | Moderate | Injury or ill-health needing medical treatment | Laceration needing stitches; minor fracture; concussion |
| 2 | Minor | Injury needing first aid only | Grazes, bruises, mild cramp |
| 1 | Negligible | Negligible injury | Brief discomfort |

**Likelihood**: how likely the harm is.

| Level | Likelihood | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Rare | Not expected to occur but still possible |
| 2 | Remote | Not likely under normal circumstances |
| 3 | Occasional | Possible or known to occur |
| 4 | Frequent | Common occurrence |
| 5 | Almost certain | Continual or repeating |

**Risk Prioritisation Number (RPN) = Severity × Likelihood.**

| Severity ↓ / Likelihood → | Rare (1) | Remote (2) | Occasional (3) | Frequent (4) | Almost certain (5) |
| --- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Catastrophic (5)** | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">5</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">10</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--high">15</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--high">20</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--high">25</span> |
| **Major (4)** | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">4</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">8</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">12</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--high">16</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--high">20</span> |
| **Moderate (3)** | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--low">3</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">6</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">9</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">12</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--high">15</span> |
| **Minor (2)** | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--low">2</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">4</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">6</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">8</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">10</span> |
| **Negligible (1)** | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--low">1</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--low">2</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--low">3</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">4</span> | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">5</span> |

This portal uses **Low = 1 to 3, Medium = 4 to 12, High = 15 to 25**. The Code lets each organisation set its own boundaries with management's agreement, so if your organisation already uses different bands, use those consistently.

| Risk level | Acceptable? | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--low">Low</span> | Acceptable | No extra controls may be needed. Keep monitoring so the risk does not creep up. |
| <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">Medium</span> | Tolerable | Reduce the risk as low as reasonably practicable within a set time. Interim controls are allowed while longer-term ones are put in place. Management attention needed. |
| <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--high">High</span> | Not acceptable | **Do not start** until the risk is reduced to at least Medium. Eliminate the hazard if you can. Do not rely mainly on protective equipment. Management review required. |

## 4. Control the risk

Choose controls from the top of the **hierarchy of control** first. Higher controls work at the source and do not depend on people remembering to do the right thing. Combining several controls usually works best.

| Order | Control | Sport examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Eliminate** the hazard | Remove a dangerous section of a race route; cancel the open-water leg when conditions are unsafe |
| 2 | **Substitute** something less hazardous | Replace a swim leg with a run; move training to an indoor, air-conditioned hall |
| 3 | **Engineering** controls | Anchor goalposts; padding on posts; barriers between runners and traffic; shade and cooling stations |
| 4 | **Administrative** controls | Schedule outside the hottest hours; rest breaks; pre-participation questionnaire; safety briefing; marshals; work-rest rules |
| 5 | **Personal protective equipment** (last resort) | Helmets, mouthguards, buoyancy aids, high-visibility vests |

After choosing additional controls, **re-rate severity and likelihood**. The new RPN must not be higher than before, and should ideally reach Low. Name a **person responsible** and a **due date** for each control.

## 5. Implement

- Have the assessment **approved** by the person in charge of the activity or area.
- Put each control into an **action plan** with owners and dates, and check it is done before the activity starts.
- **Tell everyone affected** about the risks and controls: staff, volunteers, contractors and participants, through briefings, entry information and signage.

## 6. Record

- Keep the completed assessment and records of the controls for **at least three years** (the Code's requirement for workplaces).
- Keep all assessments together in a **risk register** that can be shown to anyone who needs to review it, including regulators and venue owners.

## 7. Review

Review the assessment, and revise it if needed:

- **at least once every three years**, and preferably every year;
- **after anyone is injured** as a result of a hazard;
- when there is a **significant change** to the activity, venue, participants or procedures;
- when **new information** about a risk becomes available.

For events, also review before **each edition**, and carry out a quick **dynamic risk assessment on the day** as conditions change, such as weather or crowd numbers.

## Worked example: 10 km community run on a park connector, Saturday 7 am

| Activity | Hazard | Possible harm | Existing controls | S | L | RPN | Additional controls | S | L | RPN | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | :---: | :---: | :---: | --- | :---: | :---: | :---: | --- |
| Running | Heat and humidity | Exertional heatstroke | Early start; water at 5 km | 4 | 3 | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">12</span> | Check WBGT from 6 am with cancel/shorten rule; water every 2.5 km; ice-water immersion tub and medic at finish; heat briefing | 4 | 2 | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">8</span> | Race director |
| Running | Sudden cardiac arrest | Death | First-aider at finish | 5 | 2 | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">10</span> | AED at start/finish and mid-route; cycling first-aider with AED; pre-participation questionnaire at registration | 5 | 1 | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">5</span> | Medical lead |
| Road crossing | Collision with vehicle | Serious injury | Route signs | 4 | 3 | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">12</span> | Reroute to avoid the crossing | 4 | 1 | <span class="ssp-chip ssp-chip--moderate">4</span> | Course manager |

*S = Severity, L = Likelihood, RPN = S × L.*

## Common mistakes

- **Copying last year's assessment** without checking what has changed.
- Rating risk **without considering who takes part**: the same route is riskier for first-timers than for club runners.
- Relying on **briefings and signs alone** when a higher control is practicable.
- **No owner or date** for additional controls, so they never happen.
- Forgetting **set-up, take-down, spectators and volunteers**.
