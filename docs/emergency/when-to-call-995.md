# When to Call 995

!!! danger "Life-threatening emergency? Call 995 now."
    995 is Singapore's emergency number for an ambulance, fire and rescue. It is free for emergencies and answered 24 hours a day. If someone is unresponsive and not breathing normally, **call 995, start CPR and get an AED**. The call taker will guide you.

## Call 995 for these

SCDF lists cardiac arrest, seizures, breathlessness, loss of consciousness, heavy bleeding, major trauma and stroke as emergencies. In sport, that includes:

| Situation | Examples in sport |
| --- | --- |
| Collapse or cardiac arrest | A player collapses and does not respond, or is not breathing normally. A runner collapses at or near the finish line. |
| Suspected heatstroke | A participant becomes confused, unsteady, aggressive or collapses during exercise in the heat. See [Heat Stress & Heat Illness](../topics/environmental/heat-stress.md). |
| Serious head injury | Loss of consciousness, a seizure, worsening drowsiness, repeated vomiting, severe or increasing headache, or weakness or tingling in arms or legs after a knock to the head. |
| Suspected neck or spinal injury | Neck pain, numbness or loss of movement after a fall, collision or diving into water. Do not move the person unless they are in danger. |
| Drowning or near-drowning | Anyone pulled from the water who is unresponsive, breathless, coughing persistently or confused. |
| Severe breathing difficulty or allergic reaction | Struggling to breathe, swelling of the face or throat, an asthma attack not relieved by their inhaler. |
| Major bleeding or trauma | Bleeding that will not stop with firm pressure, a suspected broken thigh bone, or a serious collision with a vehicle on a road event. |
| Chest pain or stroke signs | Chest pain or tightness during exercise, sudden face drooping, arm weakness or slurred speech. |

!!! tip "If in doubt, call"
    The 995 Operations Centre assesses every call and will advise you. It is better to call and be told it is not an emergency than to wait.

## What to tell the call taker

1. **Your name and a phone number** to call you back on.
2. **Exactly where you are**: the address and postal code, or the nearest landmark, lamp post number or bus stop number. At a stadium, park or beach, name the gate, pitch, court or carpark.
3. **What happened and how the person is**: age, sex, what you can see. For example: *"Man, about 45, collapsed after the 10 km run, not responding, not breathing normally."*
4. **Follow the instructions** you are given and do not hang up until told to.
5. **Send someone to meet the ambulance** at the entrance and guide the crew in.
6. **Call 995 again** if the person gets worse.

!!! info "No address nearby? Use the myResponder app"
    The **Call 995** button in SCDF's myResponder app sends your location to the 995 Operations Centre. This helps on trails, beaches, park connectors and open fields with no landmarks. The app also shows nearby public AEDs.

## While you wait

- **Not breathing normally:** start chest compressions straight away and send someone for the nearest AED. Switch it on and follow its voice prompts. The call taker can coach you through CPR.
- **Suspected heatstroke:** stop the activity and **start cooling immediately**, ideally by immersing the body in cold or iced water up to the neck. Do not wait for the ambulance to start cooling.
- **Suspected spinal injury:** keep the person still and support their head in line with the body.
- **Bleeding:** press firmly on the wound with a clean cloth.
- **Keep the area clear** and stop play nearby so the crew can reach the person.

## Not an emergency? Use these instead

SCDF does not send an emergency ambulance for non-emergencies, and a charge of $274 applies if SCDF conveys a non-emergency case to hospital. Examples of non-emergencies include toothache, diarrhoea, coughs, headaches, slight burns and medical check-ups. In sport, a sprained ankle, minor cuts or muscle cramps that ease with rest are usually not emergencies.

| Need | Where to go |
| --- | --- |
| Advice on where to seek care | **NurseFirst helpline 6262 6262** |
| Minor injury or illness | A GP clinic, polyclinic or 24-hour urgent care centre |
| Non-emergency ambulance transport | **1777 until 31 December 2026.** From 1 January 2027 the 1777 hotline ceases; book a private ambulance operator directly (contacts at go.gov.sg/pao), or use a taxi or ride-hailing service if the person can travel safely |
| Police emergency | **999** |

## For organisers and operators

- Put the **venue address, postal code and nearest landmark** on your Emergency Action Plan, safety briefing and staff radios, so any caller can give it instantly.
- **Name who calls 995** and who meets the ambulance, for every session and every zone of a large event.
- Mark **ambulance access routes** and keep them clear.
- Know where your **nearest AED** is and check it works before each activity.
- Remove 1777 from emergency cards and posters by 1 January 2027.
